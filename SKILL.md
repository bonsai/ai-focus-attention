# AI Focus and Attention

## Overview

AI tools make it possible to advance multiple projects in parallel. Human deep attention capacity does not scale the same way. The highest leverage still comes from sustained concentration on a single important problem. Continuous contact with frontier models does not diminish genuine technical wonder; the frontier remains full of unsolved problems.

## Core Principles

### Attention is the scarce resource
- AI assistance expands the surface area of what one person can touch, but does not expand the depth of simultaneous deep thinking.
- Parallelizing many shallow threads is usually inferior to concentrating on one high-value, difficult problem.
- Protect and deliberately allocate deep attention rather than filling every available slot with concurrent workstreams.

### Prefer concentrated impact over parallel throughput
- When choosing what to work on, optimize for the expected value of solving one hard problem thoroughly.
- Use AI tools to accelerate the focused path (research, coding, evaluation, writing) rather than to maximize the number of open projects.
- Periodically ruthlessly prune secondary threads that dilute attention from the primary bet.

### Maintain calibrated wonder and realism
- Daily exposure to strong models does not make progress feel ordinary; new results continue to update intuitions.
- Explicitly track remaining open problems at the frontier rather than assuming the hard parts are already solved.
- Treat the continued existence of large unsolved challenges as both motivating and strategically informative.

## Practical Guidance

When planning personal or team work in an AI-augmented setting:
1. Identify the single most important problem that would create disproportionate progress if solved.
2. Structure the day and week to protect deep blocks for that problem.
3. Use AI tools aggressively inside the focused work (code, experiments, literature, debugging) rather than to spawn new parallel efforts.
4. Review open threads regularly and close or defer those that compete for attention without clear path to the main goal.
5. Revisit frontier open problems periodically to keep intuition and motivation calibrated.

## Anti-patterns
- Starting many parallel AI-assisted projects and feeling productive while making little progress on any one hard problem.
- Treating multitasking capacity as a goal rather than a side effect of better tools.
- Allowing constant model novelty to create a sense that "everything important is already done."
- Spreading attention so thinly that no single constraint is ever truly relaxed.
